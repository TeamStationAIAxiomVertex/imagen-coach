## Editorial ontology

### Central concept

**Seguridad profesional** is the ability to recognize one's current value and sustain decisions, visibility, responsibility, and external expression at the level already reached professionally.

It is not defined as looking strong, performing confidence, adopting fixed postures, wearing a sophisticated persona, or following a universal visual formula.

### Connected concepts

| Concept | Sonia's relationship | Editorial boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identidad profesional | A person's professional reality may grow faster than their internal perception of who they are. | Do not reduce identity to branding or wardrobe. |
| Perception | How a person perceives themselves influences how they show themselves. | Do not present this as a clinical diagnosis or universal scientific law. |
| Value | Value must first be recognized before it can be expressed and sustained in decisions. | Do not promise income, status, or confidence outcomes. |
| Imagen | Image is a tool that makes value visible; it does not create value. | Never describe image as a disguise, formula, or source of personal worth. |
| Imagen estratégica | Visual choices should serve identity, work, context, and the stage being built. | Do not turn strategic image into a dress code or a universal executive look. |
| Presencia y visibilidad | Growth can require occupying more space, receiving attention, and allowing one's criterion to be recognized. | Do not equate visibility with volume, performance, or constant exposure. |
| Decisiones | Security becomes observable in what a person charges, accepts, communicates, wears, chooses, and declines. | Keep decisions concrete; do not replace them with motivational abstractions. |
| Capacidad para sostener | Growth includes sustaining a price, attention, difficult conversations, responsibility, visibility, and recognition. | Do not convert this into therapy, nervous-system treatment, or neuroscience unless Sonia supplies and approves that source. |
| Dinero | Money can reveal the relationship a person has with value, price, guilt, receiving, and overwork. | Do not force every money question into abundance language or financial advice. |
| Siguiente nivel | The next stage may begin when a person stops acting from an earlier professional identity. | Do not promise transformation, promotion, revenue, or guaranteed results. |

### Relationship chain

Future articles in this cluster should preserve this causal order when the source supports it:

`percepción propia -> reconocimiento del valor -> decisiones -> expresión visible -> capacidad para sostener -> siguiente etapa profesional`

Image belongs inside that chain as visible expression. It is not the origin of value.

## Claim boundaries

### Claims allowed when supported by Sonia's source

- A person's professional reality can change before their self-perception catches up.
- Image can reveal a distance between identity, perception, decisions, and external expression.
- Security is visible in concrete decisions, not only in stated confidence.
- Image is a tool that can express value and professional evolution more clearly.
- Growth can require the capacity to sustain visibility, responsibility, prices, attention, and recognition.
- Questions about money can also be questions about value, receiving, guilt, and overwork.
- Sonia's work examines the whole structure: identity, perception, image, presence, decisions, and capacity to sustain.

### Claims blocked without a traceable Sonia source and explicit approval

- Clinical, therapeutic, neurological, nervous-system, trauma, or mental-health claims.
- Scientific, research-backed, or evidence-proven claims without a cited source Sonia supplied.
- Guaranteed emotional, financial, leadership, visibility, client, promotion, or business results.
- Statistics, percentages, timelines, client counts, audience counts, or conversion claims.
- Credentials, certifications, titles, memberships, dates, client names, or experience claims not proven by the approved source packet.
- Statements that all people, all women, all executives, or all industries behave the same way.
- New first-person beliefs attributed to Sonia when she did not write or approve them.
- New scenarios, professions, services, or techniques added only to capture keywords.

## Recurring article structure

This is a pattern, not a rigid template. The number of sections must follow the source, not an SEO quota.

1. **Tension-led title:** name the gap between current capacity and visible expression.
2. **Scope subtitle:** name the connected ideas without promising an outcome.
3. **Human observation:** begin with a recognizable professional situation.
4. **Short symptom sequence:** isolate actions or reactions in brief paragraphs.
5. **First-person thesis:** let Sonia identify where the real conversation begins.
6. **Definition through contrast:** explain what the concept is not before explaining what it is.
7. **Diagnostic questions:** help the reader locate themselves in the subject.
8. **Concrete decisions:** show where the concept appears in work, money, image, visibility, and boundaries.
9. **Key distinction:** separate image from value, confidence from capacity, or arrival from the ability to inhabit the next stage.
10. **Integration:** reconnect identity, perception, image, presence, decisions, and capacity to sustain.
11. **Reflective CTA:** invite a first conversation from the reader's real tension, without pressure or inflated promises.

## Paragraph rhythm

- Preserve short standalone sentences when Sonia uses them to create emphasis.
- Preserve one-line sequences such as actions, questions, or decisions. Do not compress them into dense prose.
- Follow short sequences with medium explanatory paragraphs that connect meaning.
- Let repetition carry the argument when Sonia repeats a structure such as `En...`, `Puedes...`, `Sostener...`, or `¿Puedes...?`.
- Keep paragraph length uneven and intentional. Do not force equal cards, equal sections, or mechanical three-paragraph blocks.
- Do not add a generic summary at the start or repeat the conclusion at the end.
- Use headings as conceptual turns, not as keyword containers.

## First-person stance

Sonia's authority appears through professional judgment, including phrases such as:

- `para mí`
- `cuando trabajo imagen estratégica`
- `yo no creo`
- `prefiero hablar`
- `no me interesa trabajar`
- `te ayudo a lograr`

Rules:

1. First person belongs only where Sonia wrote or approved the stance.
2. Do not replace first person with anonymous guide language such as `esta guía explica`.
3. Do not invent personal stories, beliefs, observations, methods, or promises for Sonia.
4. Keep first person connected to what she observes, distinguishes, asks, or helps a client examine.

## Question use

- Questions are diagnostic tools, not decorative FAQs.
- Use question sequences at moments where the reader must examine identity, value, price, visibility, recognition, or decisions.
- Keep each question focused on one observable tension.
- Do not answer every question immediately; the sequence itself helps the reader recognize a pattern.
- Do not convert Sonia's question sequences into accordions, FAQ schema, or search snippets if that changes the reading experience or wording.
- A closing question may open the CTA, but it must reflect the article's central tension.

## Authority model

Sonia's E-E-A-T signal comes from the quality of her distinctions and the consistency of her professional point of view:

1. She begins with a pattern she observes in real professional transitions.
2. She separates concepts that are often confused.
3. She names concrete decisions where the pattern becomes visible.
4. She explains what she believes, what she does not believe, and how she works.
5. She does not inflate authority through unsupported credentials, guarantees, or borrowed scientific language.
6. The page may support provenance with an accurate byline, author profile, canonical URL, publication date, and source-backed schema. Those elements must not be inserted into Sonia's prose.

## CTA pattern

The preferred CTA has four movements:

1. Ask whether the reader recognizes the central tension.
2. Describe the situation without diagnosing or blaming them.
3. Reassemble the full structure they may need to examine.
4. Offer a low-pressure beginning, using language equivalent to `podemos comenzar por ahí` only when Sonia approves it.

Avoid urgency, scarcity, transformation promises, `agenda ya`, `descubre tu mejor versión`, and other generic conversion language.

## SEO anchor placement

SEO must support Sonia's words; it must not rewrite them.

1. Choose internal links only after the article copy is approved.
2. Use a phrase that already exists naturally in the approved text.
3. Place the link at the first meaningful occurrence in body prose, not in the H1, subtitle, diagnostic question sequence, or closing CTA.
4. Link one phrase to one semantically exact destination. Do not stretch a phrase to a broader or different service.
5. Do not repeat the same exact-match anchor more than once in an article.
6. Prefer three to six contextual internal links in a long article. Add more only when each link resolves a distinct reader question.
7. Do not add a city, audience, service, or comparison phrase that is absent from the approved article.
8. Keep author, breadcrumb, Article schema, canonical, Open Graph, and related-reading signals outside Sonia's prose.
9. If inserting a link changes punctuation, word order, emphasis, or meaning, return the linked version to Sonia for approval.
10. Approved verbatim copy without link authorization remains unlinked.

## Future-article production contract

### Required inputs before drafting

- One named reader question or search intent.
- One approved Sonia source packet.
- A claim ledger mapping every substantive claim to that packet.
- The relevant service, audience, and geographic context only when the source supports them.
- Existing internal routes verified before proposing anchors.

### Draft gate

A future draft may proceed only when:

- Its central concept exists in Sonia's corpus.
- Its claims stay inside the documented boundaries.
- Its first-person statements are authored or approved by Sonia.
- Its structure follows the source rather than a preset SEO outline.
- It contains no generic filler, invented examples, or visible technical residue.

### Sonia approval gate

1. Send one clean, readable review version.
2. Separate editorial copy from metadata, schema, SEO notes, and implementation details.
3. Record Sonia's exact corrections.
4. Freeze the approved text and checksum it.
5. Do not publish, link, reformat, or optimize the frozen text beyond the approved instruction.

### Publication gate

Publication remains blocked until all are true:

- Sonia approved the exact reader-facing version.
- Any internal-link insertions were approved or explicitly authorized.
- Metadata and schema make no claims absent from the approved source.
- The rendered page preserves headings, paragraph rhythm, questions, and CTA.
- Mobile and desktop renders contain no truncation, overlap, raw markup, duplicated headings, or missing text.
- The Markdown/agent version preserves the same approved wording and provenance.

## Fail-closed rule

When source, claim, voice, approval, link authorization, or rendering evidence is uncertain, stop. Do not fill the gap with plausible copy. Return the missing evidence or the smallest draft-only blocker.
